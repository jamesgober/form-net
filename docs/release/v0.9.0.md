# form-net v0.9.0 - Polish

**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Compare:** [`v0.8.0...v0.9.0`](https://github.com/jamesgober/form-net/compare/v0.8.0...v0.9.0)

**Every gap accepted earlier in the series, tried again.** v0.9.0 is the polish pass that comes before any 1.0 candidate (D-112): each accepted gap, deferred item, known issue, and missed target was reopened, and is now fixed or listed with its numbers and its reason in [`dev/READINESS-1.0.md`](../../dev/READINESS-1.0.md). The security review was run again over the whole crate; its three findings are fixed test-first.

## What changed

- **Borrowed raw decode** (D-126): `RawPair::decode_name` and `decode_value` return a component with nothing to decode borrowed from the body, not copied. `raw_pairs` on 1,000 fields goes from 0.72x form_urlencoded to 1.49x.
- **Byte-wise name lookup** (D-127): `Form::get`, `get_one`, `get_all`, and `contains` compare names as bytes, 7 to 3.3 ns per field.
- **One text rule for multipart fields** (D-128): every entry point checks a text field's charset and UTF-8 by the same rule, before `Inspector::field`, whether or not an inspector skipped the part. A skipped `_charset_` field still declares the charset of the fields after it, so a skip cannot change how the rest of the body reads.
- **`csrf.token_skipped`** (D-129): `TokenCheck` refuses with 500 when another inspector skipped the token's part, so a pair of inspectors cannot let a body through unverified.

## Fixed

- A urlencoded body made only of separators could allocate past `Limits::memory_bound`; the arena is now bounded by the body, the limit, and the text the body can hold (D-130).
- `Limits::memory_bound` counted 256 bytes of per-request state; the real figure is 1 KiB, and a test holds every per-request struct under it (D-131).

## Verification

- `tests/skip_parity.rs`: every body reads the same whether an inspector skips a part or not; it failed on 0.8.0 before the fix.
- `tests/memory_bound.rs`: separator-only and separator-heavy bodies stay under `memory_bound`.
- The multer differential rerun: 2,119 mutants, none read differently.
- 30 minutes of fuzzing on every target after the fixes, no findings.
- Local gate on Windows and WSL Ubuntu, stable and 1.85; CI green on every platform before the tag.

## Performance

A fresh baseline against the competitors at their current versions (`benches/baseline/2026-11-08`, D-132). The machine stayed at full load, so each number is the median of five runs of the interleaved harness, which puts the load on both sides; absolute times read high for every crate and the 0.7.0 quiet numbers stay the reference for them.

- Every family passes, and allocation counts are unchanged. The two ratios that read lower than 0.7.0 were measured against 0.8.0 on the same machine: one is the load, the other code placement under the bench profile, with 0.9.0 level or ahead under the release profile (D-133).
- `raw_pairs` + `decode_into`: 1,000 fields 0.72x to 1.49x form_urlencoded, the search query 0.91x to 1.55x.
- `Form::get`: 3.4 ns per field, was 7.
- Near-miss delimiters on a whole 1 MiB body: 1.39x multer, was 1.23x.
- Still level: the 25-byte search query through `pairs`, 0.94x collecting and 1.05x iterating (D-111); the reason and the choice for 1.0 are in the readiness note.

## Not in this release

The v0.9.x exit needs two weeks of use by web-net and customs-sec in a test deployment (D-125); both are still scaffolds. The adversarial review, `docs/STABILITY-1.0.md`, and the high-byte header idea are in v0.9.x.

## What's next

`0.9.x` release candidates once web-net and customs-sec run the routes in `tests/alpha_routes.rs`. 1.0 comes after that, on James's decision.
